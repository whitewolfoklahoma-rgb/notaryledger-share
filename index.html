How NotaryLedger works

The idea

One file, no account, no subscription, no order-import middleman — your book never leaves this device. Log every appointment once: fee, acts, miles, signer, and who owes you. The app ages unpaid fees, builds your appointment record, and turns the same entries into quarterly tax estimates and a Schedule C summary. NotaryGadget and NotaryAssist charge $8.99–$11.95 every month for this job; this file is yours outright.

The notarial-fee exemption (the big one)

Under long-standing IRS rules (see IRS Publication 17 and the Schedule SE instructions), fees received for performing notarial acts are exempt from self-employment tax. They are not exempt from regular income tax. That's why every job tracks its notarial-fee portion separately: the app subtracts notarial-fee portions collected from your profit before computing SE tax, but keeps the full profit in the income-tax estimate. Over a year of signings this is routinely hundreds of dollars — and the most common reason agents overpay. This app applies the standard treatment as a planning estimate; it is not tax advice. State rules and individual situations vary — confirm with a tax professional.

Formulas used

Net profit (cash basis) = fees collected (paid jobs only) − deductible expenses − mileage deduction. Meals count at 50%. Mileage deduction = business miles × the standard mileage rate saved for that year (Settings). SE-taxable profit = max(0, net profit − notarial-fee portions collected). Self-employment tax = SE-taxable profit × 92.35% × 15.3%. Federal estimate = (full net profit − half the SE tax) × your federal effective-rate %. State estimate = net profit × your state %. Each quarter's suggested payment is one quarter of the cumulative tax on annualized year-to-date profit.

What's NOT in these estimates

This is a planning worksheet, not a tax return and not tax advice. It leaves out: the Social Security wage-base cap on part of the SE tax, the standard deduction and real tax brackets (that's why you supply an effective rate), other income, W-2 wages, credits, retirement-plan deductions, and state quirks. If your profit is large or your situation is mixed, a tax professional will beat this worksheet — use it to avoid surprises and underpayment penalties, not as your final number.

Cash basis — why unpaid fees aren't income yet

Most solo notaries are cash-basis taxpayers: a fee is income when the money arrives. Unpaid jobs live in Jobs & Payments as receivables (with aging) and join your profit — and your estimates — when you mark them paid. If your business files on the accrual method instead, this app's profit timing won't match your return; talk to your preparer.

The Journal view

The Journal tab arranges your own job entries as an appointment record (signer, ID type, document, acts). Journal law is state-specific: some states require no journal, some require one with prescribed contents and retention, a few require electronic or state-vendor journals or thumbprints. This record does not replace a journal your state requires. Know your state's rule and keep the mandated journal exactly as prescribed.

Mileage vs. actual car costs

Pick one method per vehicle per year. If you use the standard mileage rate (what this app's Mileage tab does), you may still log parking fees and tolls under "Car & truck" — but not gas, repairs, or insurance for that vehicle. Logging both inflates your deduction; the IRS disallows it. When a job's miles are also logged as a trip, the trip stays in the Mileage log even if the job is later deleted — delete the trip separately if you need to, so your records stay explainable.

Your data

Everything is stored in this browser's local storage on this device only. Clearing your browser's site data erases it — export backups from Settings & Backup and keep the file somewhere safe. A backup from this app can be re-imported into any copy of NotaryLedger.

Defaults to localize

Four numbers deserve your attention in Settings: your state's per-act notarial fee (drives the notarial-fee portion auto-fill), the mileage rates (IRS sets them per year by notice — verify yours), your federal/state effective rates (last year's return — tax ÷ income — is a good source), and your commission expiry (the Dashboard warns you 60 days out).
